# Facts: what is true about HOUND, and how to word things

Use only these facts. Do not invent performance numbers, tests, users, partners or deployments. If you need a
fact that is not here, read the sources at the bottom; if it is not there either, leave it out or mark it as an
assumption.

## What HOUND is

A student-built search-and-rescue rover (team: Rishith Chennupati and teammates). A rescuer drives it from a laptop
into a space that is not safe for people. When it stops, it senses for people:

- **Wi-Fi sensing.** One ESP32-S3 sends 100 Wi-Fi probes a second; a second ESP32-S3 on the rover measures how
  each arrives on 51 subcarriers (channel state information, CSI). A small neural network on the Raspberry Pi reads
  a 10 second window and answers PERSON or NO ONE, and still or moving.
- **24 GHz radar.** An HLK-LD2450 tracks a moving person (x, y, speed). Two HLK-LD2410C radars give range and look
  for the slow repeating motion of breathing.
- **Thermal camera.** An MLX90640, 32 by 24 pixels.
- **Also:** a USB webcam, two VL53L1X distance sensors.
- **Rover:** aluminium extrusion frame, four NEMA 17 steppers on 80 mm mecanum wheels, Arduino UNO R4 with DRV8825
  drivers, Raspberry Pi 4, 3S 2200 mAh LiPo. The Arduino stops the motors by itself 400 ms after the Pi goes quiet.
- The rover hardware and its web remote exist. **This console (HOUND Command) is not connected to the rover.**
  The rover firmware has no Bluetooth; the console's Bluetooth button can only talk to a device that advertises
  the Nordic UART service under a name starting with HOUND.

## The Wi-Fi neural network (the real AI in this project)

- A 1-D CNN written from scratch in NumPy, **23,372 parameters**: three convolutions (52→24→24→24 channels,
  9 taps), pooling, mean and max over time, a dense layer (48→32), two heads (person; still/moving).
  Every gradient was written by hand and checked against finite differences.
- NumPy rather than PyTorch because the Pi already has NumPy and PyTorch is a 700 MB install.
- **Trained and tested only on simulated people.** A Blender script builds 900 people (1.20 to 1.95 m, 70 %
  standing, 30 % lying) behind a wall; a channel simulator computes the Wi-Fi signal (direct path, 3 to 8 room
  echoes, each body point a reflector, breathing 7 to 30 per minute, walking 0.3 to 1.0 m/s, a fan in a third of
  empty rooms) and then adds the ESP32's own faults (gain jumps, drift, noise, lost packets, jitter).
- **No real recordings exist yet.** Never write or imply that the network was tested on real people.
- Data: 4,384 training windows, 961 validation, **955 test windows**, split by person (the test people were never
  seen in training). Training takes 23 s on a laptop CPU.
- The PERSON threshold (0.53) was set on validation so that at most 10 % of empty rooms raise an alarm.

Results on the 955 simulated test windows:

| | |
|---|---|
| A moving person behind the wall is found | **86.9 %** |
| Still or moving, when someone is there | **91.5 %** |
| An empty room is called empty | 88.9 % (false alarm 11.1 %; with a fan 7.9 %, without 13.0 %) |
| A still person (breathing only) is found | **21.4 %** (standing 25.5 %, lying 12.4 %) |
| Overall accuracy on "is someone there?" | 59.3 % (confusion: 240 / 30 / 359 / 326) |
| One reading on a laptop | 1.1 ms (window + network); 0.1 ms for the network alone. The Pi has not been timed. |

What the experiments showed (these are the AI design decisions, with evidence):

- **The simulator has to be messy.** The same network trained on clean simulated signals and tested on the messy
  ones calls 81 % of empty rooms a person, and its still/moving answer is a coin toss (48 %).
- **A still person through a wall is the hard case.** Breathing changes the signal by about 0.05 to 0.4 %; the
  ESP32's own noise is about 0.5 % per bin. This is why HOUND fuses the network with radar and thermal.
- **Breathing rate is not reliable.** It is within 3 breaths/min in only 41 % of the still people found, and a clear-
  looking rhythm does not predict a right answer. **Do not display a breathing rate as a reading.** A breathing
  estimate may be shown only as an experiment, with this caveat next to it.
- **Wi-Fi amplitude cannot say left, centre or right** (30.1 % where chance is 33 %). Position comes from the radar.

In the browser: the same weights run in JavaScript (`app/core/nn.js`) and match the Python output on all 30
bundled test windows (6 each: empty room, empty room + fan, person moving, person standing still, person lying
still). Of those 30, the network is right on 20; it misses most still people, as the full test predicts.

## What is simulated in HOUND Command

- The block of buildings, the people, decoys, hazards, the rover's and drone's motion: an invented training exercise
  placed in the real valley of Timure, Nepal.
- Radar, thermal and drone readings: generated by the simulator from that scenario.
- Fusion likelihoods for radar, thermal, drone and acoustic evidence: **assumed** design values (only the Wi-Fi
  network's likelihood is measured, from its test set).
- The drone, a second rover, relay beacons, gas sensing, the phone finder: **concepts**. Only one rover exists.
- "Rescue team" movement and confirmations: simulated.

## Wording rules

- Say "simulated", "in simulation", "in this scenario". Say "concept" for things that do not exist.
- Never: "detects survivors" as a proven fact, "tested with real people", "deployed", "used by rescue teams",
  "saves lives", accuracy claims without "in simulation".
- Honest strength is the selling point: say what works (a moving person, 87 % in simulation), what does not yet
  (a still person, 21 %), and what the design does about it (fusion, a second look, a human check).
- Not medical: any triage or priority shown is a planning aid, not a medical assessment. Say so where shown.
- The exercise is set in Timure, Nepal, after the real Bhote Koshi flood. The terrain and satellite imagery are real.
  The block of buildings, the people, the damage and every reading are invented. Say this plainly wherever the
  place is named, and treat the real disaster with respect: no invented casualty details, no drama about real victims.
  The site's south-west corner is anchored at 28.25285 N, 85.36667 E.
- Plain words. Short sentences. No hype words ("revolutionary", "game-changing", "cutting-edge").
